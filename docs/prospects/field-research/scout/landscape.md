# Scout lane: landscape

Scouted 2026-10-21 by a scout subagent.

## AI idea-validation products

- **LND-01** [grounded] ValidatorAI gives a free 0–100 score on a startup idea from five criteria: customer clarity, problem specificity, founder-market fit, market size, and monetization potential. A paid one-to-one advisory service is optional. The FAQ calls the score "a starting point, not a final verdict," describes no accuracy testing of the scoring, and cites no external sources. — [ValidatorAI FAQ](https://validatorai.com/faq), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-02** [grounded] DimeADozen sells one-off validation reports delivered as PDFs ($9 Starter, $129 Entrepreneur, $179 three-pack, no subscription). The maker says the reports run to 200+ pages with 800+ URL citations, and offers a free idea score and 29 public sample reports. The maker also says an AI agent team runs the company, with a human founder holding final authority. Customer and report counts (3,100+ and 3,200+) are self-reported. Commenters asked for a side-by-side comparison mode, which does not exist yet. — [DimeADozen.AI on Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/dimeadozen-ai/dimeadozen-ai), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-03** [grounded] LEANSTACK, the company behind the Lean Canvas, sells LEANSpark. It drafts a Lean Canvas from a plain-language description of the idea, asks clarifying questions, stress-tests the canvas on seven dimensions, and recommends which assumptions to test first. The Pro plan drafts interview plans and analyzes interviews the user brings. Pricing is a free check, then $25, $50 or $100 a month. — [LEANSpark: Lean Canvas](https://leanspark.ai/leancanvas), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-04** [grounded] Preuve AI offers a startup-validation MCP server and API. They are pitched as a check that coding agents in Claude, Cursor or Codex run before they build, and as a "cofounder who says no." The README says each idea is scored on six factors (market, demand, competition, moat, business model, regulatory risk) using 60+ live sources. It reports a vendor benchmark in which the median idea scored 54/100 and 17.5% reached the "go" line. It also says Preuve is listed as an official connector in the Claude Connectors Directory and works on the free plan. This lane could not open the directory page to confirm the listing. The repository was created on 2026-10-15. — [PreuveAI/startup-idea-validation-mcp README](https://github.com/PreuveAI/startup-idea-validation-mcp), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Deep-research features of AI assistants

- **LND-05** [grounded] Claude's Research feature (announced 2025-04-15) runs several searches that build on each other across the web and Google Workspace, and returns citations meant to be easy to check. It launched as an early beta for Max, Team and Enterprise plans in the US, Japan and Brazil. — [Claude takes research to new places](https://claude.com/blog/research), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-06** [grounded] Gemini Deep Research drafts a research plan that the user can edit before the research starts. It uses Google Search by default and can add Gmail, Drive, uploaded files and NotebookLM notebooks. A report usually takes 5–10 minutes. Free users have daily and concurrent limits. The help page says nothing about accuracy or source reliability. — [Use Deep Research in Gemini Apps (Google Help)](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-07** [grounded] At the launch of ChatGPT deep research, OpenAI acknowledged that the tool can sometimes make up facts, though at a lower rate than its other models (reported 2025-02-03). The same article reports that the tool struggles to tell authoritative information from rumor and often fails to convey uncertainty. — [OpenAI's deep research hallucinates (Futurism, 2025-02-03)](https://futurism.com/openai-ai-researcher-hallucinates), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Skills, plugins, GPTs and agents in the assistant ecosystems

- **LND-08** [grounded] Anthropic's official knowledge-work-plugins repository (about 31k stars, aimed at Claude Cowork) has a product-management plugin with two related skills. `competitive-brief` researches named competitors on the web (product and pricing pages, changelogs, G2 and Capterra reviews, job posts) and writes a comparison, positioning, strengths and weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Its stated uses include sales battle cards and board materials. `product-brainstorming` has an assumption-testing mode that lists the assumptions, names the riskiest one, suggests the cheapest test, and argues the strongest case against the idea. — [anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins, product-management skills](https://github.com/anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins/tree/main/product-management/skills), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-09** [grounded] Sahil Lavingia's `validate-idea` skill (in slavingia/skills, about 10.9k stars, based on *The Minimalist Entrepreneur*) treats selling as the test of an idea. It asks who has the problem, how they cope today, and whether 10 potential customers have been spoken to and 3 would pay. It lists red and green flags and returns Validated, Needs more validation, or Pivot. The skill only questions the user and contains no web-research step. — [slavingia/skills: validate-idea SKILL.md](https://github.com/slavingia/skills/blob/main/skills/validate-idea/SKILL.md), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-10** [grounded] ferdinandobons/startup-skill (MIT, about 1.2k stars, created 2026-03-06, v1.10.0) packages four skills: startup-design, startup-competitors, startup-positioning, and startup-pitch. They install as a Claude Code plugin or as `.skill` files uploaded to Claude.ai. startup-design runs eight phases plus a pre-flight check and a customer-interview gate. It produces 30+ deliverables, including market research, competitive analysis, brand, and financial projections, and has a fast-track go/no-go mode. The README promises "If your idea should die, it will tell you." It warns that the skills use many tokens and recommends Claude Max 5x. — [ferdinandobons/startup-skill README](https://github.com/ferdinandobons/startup-skill), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-11** [grounded] whawkinsiv/solo-founder-skills (about 259 stars) is a Claude Code plugin ordered around "sell before you build". Its `validate` skill tests demand with the cheapest experiment, scores the evidence, and gives a dated verdict. `is-this-an-app` decides what form the idea should take, including no build at all. `domain-name` names the product and checks domain availability. Pricing, landing-page, build, and go-live skills follow. — [whawkinsiv/solo-founder-skills README](https://github.com/whawkinsiv/solo-founder-skills), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-12** [grounded] MaxKmet/idea-validation-agents (about 488 stars) is a repository that users clone and open in Claude Code (`CLAUDE.md`), Codex CLI (`AGENTS.md`), or Cursor (`.cursor/rules`). It routes users to idea-generation workflows (7–10 scored ideas) or validation workflows and saves results to a `memory/` folder. It targets indie and solo developers. — [MaxKmet/idea-validation-agents README](https://github.com/MaxKmet/idea-validation-agents), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-13** [grounded] Garry Tan's gstack (about 137k stars, created 2026-03-11) includes an `office-hours` skill billed as YC Office Hours. Startup mode asks six forcing questions: demand reality, status quo, desperate specificity, narrowest wedge, observation, and future-fit. Builder mode is for side projects, hackathons, learning and open source. The skill saves a design doc and should run on its own when a user asks whether something is worth building, before the plan-review skills. — [garrytan/gstack: office-hours SKILL.md](https://github.com/garrytan/gstack/blob/main/office-hours/SKILL.md), accessed 2026-10-21.

For "idea validator" the counts are 14, 195, and 801. The `startup-validation` topic has 47 repositories. ## AI-native development frameworks with a discovery phase

- **LND-15** [grounded] GitHub Spec Kit (about 142k stars) ships an opt-in `assess` extension, added 2026-07-17 in PR #3568. It decides whether an idea deserves investment before any spec is written, and needs no existing code. It has five stages (intake, research, define, shape, decide), each written to Markdown under `.specify/assessments/<slug>/`. It ends with a verdict of `go`, `needs-clarification`, or `kill`, and a `go` can pass a summary to `/speckit-specify`. The rules: research must include evidence against the idea, unsourced claims are tagged `ASSUMPTION`, and a `go` needs evidence rated at least adequate. The README counts stopping with a documented reason as a useful outcome. The examples use GitHub Copilot's syntax, and other agents use their own invocation syntax. — [Spec Kit README: Idea assessment](https://github.com/github/spec-kit#idea-assessment); [Spec Kit agentic assessment reference](https://github.github.io/spec-kit/reference/agentic-assessment.html); [extensions/assess commit history](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/commits/main/extensions/assess), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-16** [grounded] The BMAD Method (about 54k stars) has an optional explore-and-validate step before planning, and its workflows can run in any order. They include brainstorming; Forge Idea, which questions an idea one question at a time and ends Hardened, Killed or Clearer, and which the docs say "does not agree or praise"; deep recon research; and a product brief or PRFAQ that feeds the PRD. The deep-recon skill offers market, domain, technical, competitive, user-voice and academic-literature research. Its rules forbid concluding from training data alone and require a publisher, publication date and access date for each claim. It treats absence of evidence as a finding, and it can instead draft a prompt for the user's own deep-research tool. — [BMAD: Explore and validate an idea](https://docs.bmad-method.org/plan/explore-and-validate-an-idea/); [bmad-deep-recon SKILL.md](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD/blob/main/skills/bmad-deep-recon/SKILL.md), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-17** [grounded] Kiro's spec workflow (introduced 2025-07-14) goes from a single prompt to requirements in EARS notation, then a design document, then tasks. The launch post describes no market, viability or discovery step before requirements. — [Introducing Kiro](https://kiro.dev/blog/introducing-kiro/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-18** [grounded] In Shape Up, a pitch has five ingredients: problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes, and no-gos. Appetite is the time a team wants to spend, as opposed to an estimate of how long the work will take. — [Shape Up, chapter 6: Write the Pitch (Basecamp)](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Non-AI methods

- **LND-19** [grounded] The Lean Canvas is Ash Maurya's one-page framework for capturing a business idea. Atlassian's guide lists its drawbacks: no detailed customer validation, little attention to competition or shifting markets, it covers the "what" but not the "how", and it oversimplifies businesses with multiple revenue streams or heavy regulation. — [Lean Canvas guide (Atlassian)](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/resources/guides/how-to/lean-canvas), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-20** [grounded] *The Mom Test* is a handbook on asking customers questions that yield real data rather than polite answers. Its site sums up the premise as "everyone is lying to you" about your idea. — [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-21** [grounded] Teresa Torres's opportunity solution tree maps a desired outcome to opportunities (customer needs and pains), then to solutions, then to assumption tests. It is a living document for a product trio, refreshed every three to four customer interviews. — [Opportunity Solution Trees (Product Talk)](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-22** [grounded] Amazon's PR/FAQ is written before building. It pairs a launch-style press release with external FAQs (customers, press) and internal FAQs (economics, risks, feasibility). A first draft should take hours, not days. A PR/FAQ may not be approved when the idea is too similar to existing products, the market is too small, the investment is high for a risky payoff, the product isn't viable yet, or it ranks below the existing backlog. — [Working Backwards PR/FAQ process](https://workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-23** [grounded] In Gary Klein's project premortem, team members "assume that the project they are planning has just failed" and list plausible reasons why. Its stated purpose is to make it safe for knowledgeable dissenters to speak up. — [Performing a Project Premortem (HBR, 2007)](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-24** [grounded] Six Thinking Hats is a parallel-thinking process in which a group takes one stance at a time: white (facts), yellow (positives), black (risks), red (feelings), green (creativity), and blue (process). — [Six Thinking Hats (de Bono Group)](https://www.debonogroup.com/services/core-programs/six-thinking-hats/), accessed 2026-10-21.

## "Do nothing": just asking a chatbot

- **LND-25** [grounded] Anthropic researchers found that five leading AI assistants were consistently sycophantic across four free-form tasks (2023-10-23, ICLR 2024). Human preference data favored answers that matched the user's views. Both people and preference models sometimes preferred a convincing sycophantic answer to a correct one. — [Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models (arXiv 2310.13548)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-26** [grounded] In April 2025, OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update after the model became overly validating and praised dangerous decisions and ideas. OpenAI attributed this to training that weighted short-term user feedback too heavily, and promised changes to training, system prompts and evaluations. — [OpenAI explains why ChatGPT became too sycophantic (TechCrunch, 2025-04-29)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-27** [grounded] In an experiment with 1,500 participants across 30 decision environments, AI advice generally moved people away from their initial views, even though the tested model was measurably sycophantic. More sycophancy weakened the effect (submitted 2026-07-30). — [AI Sycophancy and Decisions (arXiv 2607.28133)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28133), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-28** [grounded] In a field experiment, 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs got either a GPT-4-based AI mentor or a placebo guide. Owners who were above the median before the study improved performance by about 15%. Those below the median saw revenues and profits fall by about 10%. The researchers' best guess is that struggling owners asked harder questions, and the AI's plausible-looking advice did not solve them. — [What happens when business owners turn to chatbots for advice (HBS Working Knowledge)](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-happens-when-business-owners-turn-to-chatbots-for-advice), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Gaps in what exists

- **LND-29** [grounded] Microsoft Research audited generative search engines and deep-research setups, including GPT-4.5/5, You.com, Perplexity, Copilot/Bing and Gemini. All gave one-sided answers to debate questions and produced large shares of statements their own sources did not support. Citation accuracy ranged from 40% to 80% across systems. — [DeepTRACE (arXiv 2509.04499)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-30** [grounded] Across 10 models and agents, 3–13% of citation URLs had no Wayback Machine record and probably never existed, and 5–18% did not resolve. Deep-research agents produced more citations than search-augmented LLMs but invented links at higher rates. A URL-checking tool cut non-resolving links to under 1% (submitted 2026-04-03). — [Detecting and Correcting Reference Hallucinations (arXiv 2604.03173)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03173), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-31** [grounded] A blog post by the founder of Preuve AI, a validation vendor, says ChatGPT tends to look for reasons an idea could work, invents market sizes (one case: $4.2 billion for a market nearer $400 million), and gives citations that often lead to 404s. It relays two 2026 third-party tests. In one, ChatGPT recommended building all three submitted ideas while a data-backed validator rejected two. In the other, a score for the same idea moved between 65 and 78 across re-prompts. — [How to Use ChatGPT to Validate a Startup Idea (Preuve AI blog)](https://preuve.ai/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-validate-a-startup-idea), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **LND-32** [grounded] Spec Kit's `assess` decide scorecard rates six criteria: problem validity, evidence strength, value against the cost of doing nothing, feasibility and appetite, strategic fit, and risk posture. None covers monetization or revenue. Its research stage fetches without asking only from a fixed allowlist (github.com, gitlab.com, bitbucket.org, Atlassian, Linear, Notion, Google Docs, Stack Overflow and Stack Exchange). Other hosts need the user's confirmation, which defaults to no, and are skipped in automated runs. — [speckit.assess.decide.md](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/main/extensions/assess/commands/speckit.assess.decide.md); [speckit.assess.research.md](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/main/extensions/assess/commands/speckit.assess.research.md), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Notes for frame

_Judgment, not evidence._

- The two closest analogues are dev-framework front ends: Spec Kit `assess` (LND-15, LND-32) and BMAD's explore step (LND-16). Both triage an idea before specs, require cited evidence, treat "kill" as a valid result, and hand off to a build workflow. Spec Kit's scorecard has no monetization criterion. This lane did not check BMAD for one.
- Community idea-validation skills for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor are numerous and multiplying fast (LND-09 to LND-14). The existence of "a skill that validates ideas" is not open ground. Any opening would come from posture, surface (chat Project plus code), or workflow fit.
- Most products market a harsh or forcing posture (LND-04, LND-10, LND-13, LND-15). The intent's opt-in, constructive challenge is a different stance. Whether builders want it is a question for the Demand lane.
- The evidence on "just ask a chatbot" points both ways (LND-25, LND-26 against; LND-27 partly for; LND-28 mixed). Doing nothing remains a credible substitute.
- Citation reliability in deep research is a documented weak spot (LND-07, LND-29, LND-30). It bears on how much a cited ledger can be trusted without checking each link.
- Spec Kit `assess` is about three months old and the GitHub counts climb steeply in 2026 (LND-14, LND-15). Re-scout this lane within a few months.
