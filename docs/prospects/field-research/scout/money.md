# Scout lane: money

Scouted 2026-10-21 by a scout subagent.

## AI idea-validation products

- **MON-01** [grounded] DimeADozen sells idea-validation reports as one-time purchases with no subscription: a free idea score, a low-cost starter report, a full report bought with a credit that never expires, multi-report packs, and paid add-ons (investor prep, a market-test kit). The only recurring plans are for agencies, through a separate white-label product. The pricing page describes no on-site advertising, though its privacy FAQ says data may be shared with advertising providers. — [DimeADozen pricing](https://www.dimeadozen.ai/pricing), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-02** [grounded] IdeaProof sells one-time credit packs ("no subscriptions, credits never expire"). New accounts get free credits for one validation, and each feature costs a set number of credits: validation, market analysis, business plan, brand strategy, visual identity, marketing suite. No advertising is described. — [IdeaProof pricing](https://ideaproof.io/pricing), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-03** [grounded] WorthBuild's own site sells pay-per-report credits with no subscription, offers one free validation a month, and has free side tools (idea score, landing pages). Its AlternativeTo listing, added 8 March 2026, describes it instead as a monthly subscription with a limited free version. Neither page mentions advertising. — [WorthBuild](https://worthbuild.io/); [WorthBuild on AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/worthbuild/about), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-04** [grounded] ValidatorAI presents its validation tools as free and its newsletter as free. The homepage shows no paid tier, credits or advertising, and its `/pricing` URL returned 404. — [ValidatorAI](https://validatorai.com/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-05** [grounded] A competitor's comparison page, checked 12 October 2026, says IdeaBrowser now sells a single annual Pro plan bundling deep-research reports on the member's own ideas, playbooks that run over MCP, a monthly accelerator, events, tool deals and a builder community. It says the cheaper Starter tier and the coaching-heavy Empire tier have left the pricing page. IdeaBrowser's own pricing page returned HTTP 429 when opened. — [Preuve AI: IdeaBrowser comparison](https://www.preuve.ai/compare/ideabrowser), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Open-source AI development tools and frameworks

- **MON-06** [grounded] The BMAD Method is MIT-licensed and says it has "no paywalled workflows or gated community". It asks for support through Buy Me a Coffee, repository stars and corporate sponsorship by email. The name is a trademark of BMad Code, LLC. The README doesn't say whether add-on modules or video tutorials are paid. — [bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-07** [grounded] GitHub Spec Kit is MIT-licensed and lives under the `github` organization. Its README describes no paid tier, sponsorship or hosted service for the toolkit, and it installs and runs locally. — [github/spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-08** [grounded] Agent OS is MIT-licensed, and its README sends users to Builder Methods Pro for official support. Builder Methods Pro is the creator's paid annual membership, sold per individual or per team seat, covering live workshops, recordings, courses (including one on Claude Code), starter kits and a private community. The page says Builder Methods isn't owned by an AI company or tool vendor and mentions no advertising. — [buildermethods/agent-os](https://github.com/buildermethods/agent-os); [Builder Methods Pro](https://buildermethods.com/pro), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-09** [grounded] Superpowers, a Claude Code skills library, is MIT-licensed and built by the company Prime Radiant. Its README offers enterprise users commercial support, additional tooling or managed spending through a sales email, with no published prices. — [obra/superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-10** [grounded] Task Master uses the MIT license with a Commons Clause: anyone may build and sell products with it, but no one may sell Task Master itself, offer it as a hosted service, or build competing products on it. The repository links to Hamster, a hosted planning workspace sold as a free tier, a per-creator monthly Team plan and an Enterprise plan with SSO and SOC 2. Viewer and reviewer seats are free, and no advertising is mentioned. — [eyaltoledano/claude-task-master](https://github.com/eyaltoledano/claude-task-master); [Hamster pricing](https://tryhamster.com/pricing), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-11** [grounded] In Tidelift's 2024 survey of more than 400 open-source maintainers, 60% called themselves unpaid hobbyists, the same share as the year before. Among income sources, donation programs such as GitHub Sponsors reached 25% of maintainers, salaries covering maintenance 24%, Tidelift 19%, and direct company payments 5%. — [The Register, 18 Sep 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/09/18/open_source_maintainers_underpaid/), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Marketplaces for skills, GPTs and plugins

- **MON-12** [absence] No way to charge for a plugin, and no revenue share, is described in the distribution docs of the three plugin marketplaces this tool targets. Searched on 2026-10-21: Claude Code's "Create a marketplace" and "Publish and distribute a plugin" pages, claude.com's "Submit your plugin" page, GitHub Docs' "Creating a plugin marketplace for GitHub Copilot CLI", and Cursor's 17 February 2026 Marketplace announcement. Web searches for paid Claude Code plugins and for Cursor plugin revenue sharing also found none. In all three, a marketplace is a git repository with a `marketplace.json` file, and Copilot CLI also reads the `.claude-plugin/` location. — [Claude Code: create a marketplace](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces); [Claude Code: publish a plugin](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish); [claude.com: submit your plugin](https://claude.com/docs/plugins/submit); [GitHub Docs: Copilot CLI marketplace](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/copilot-cli/customize-copilot/plugins-marketplace); [Cursor: Marketplace](https://cursor.com/blog/marketplace), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-13** [grounded] You need a paid claude.ai plan to submit a plugin to Anthropic's directory. The plugin must sit in a GitHub repository, which can stay private during review but must be public before the listing goes live. — [claude.com: submit your plugin](https://claude.com/docs/plugins/submit); [Claude Code: publish a plugin](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-14** [grounded] The Claude Marketplace, launched around late September 2026, groups free connectors and plugins, partner agents and products for sale, and consulting partners. Purchases from partners "can count toward your existing Anthropic commitment". The page mentions no revenue share, commission or listing fee. One independent analyst says Anthropic hasn't published revenue-share terms, and infers that the selling side looks reserved for large partners. — [Claude Marketplace](https://claude.com/marketplace); [BleepingComputer, 27 Sep 2026](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-turns-claude-into-an-ai-marketplace-with-2-000-plus-plugins-and-connectors); [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/2026-10-04-claude-marketplace-anthropic-is-building-the-distribution-layer/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-15** [grounded] OpenAI promised a GPT Store builder revenue program, paid by engagement, for Q1 2024. An OpenAI FAQ quoted on the community forum in June 2024 says it ran as a pilot with a small group of US builders of popular GPTs, with no more builders being accepted. A December 2025 reply in the same thread says it was still being tested with US users. — [OpenAI community: GPT store revenue share status](https://community.openai.com/t/what-is-the-status-with-gpt-store-revenue-share/839172), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-16** [grounded] For apps in ChatGPT, OpenAI recommends external checkout on the developer's own domain, but approval is currently limited to physical-goods purchases. An in-ChatGPT payment sheet is in private beta for select marketplaces. The page mentions neither revenue share nor advertising. — [OpenAI Apps SDK: monetization](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/build/monetization), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-17** [grounded] Poe, Quora's bot platform, lets creators set a price per message on their bots: users spend Poe's points and creators are paid in dollars. It launched in April 2024 for US creators only. An earlier program, from October 2023, pays creators up to a fixed amount per user who subscribes to Poe because of their bots. — [TechCrunch, 9 Apr 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/09/poe-introduces-a-price-per-message-revenue-model-for-ai-bot-creators), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-18** [grounded] JetBrains Marketplace sells paid plugins and takes a 15% commission per sale. The rate can change on one month's notice, can't exceed 25%, and can be negotiated for high-revenue plugins. There's no listing fee. — [JetBrains Marketplace: revenue sharing and fees](https://plugins.jetbrains.com/docs/marketplace/revenue-sharing-and-fees.html), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-19** [grounded] Apify Store lets developers price their automation "Actors" either per event triggered in code or by usage alone, and Apify keeps 20% of an Actor's revenue as commission. — [Apify docs: monetize your Actor](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-20** [grounded] PromptBase, a prompt marketplace, charges sellers 20% on sales the marketplace brings in and 0% on sales through the seller's own link. A sample of 188 live listings found a median price of $5.99, with 91% at $10 or under. The analysis is dated 30 July 2026 and comes from the founder of a checkout platform for digital sellers, who discloses that interest. — [CartMango: how to sell AI prompts](https://cartmango.com/sell-ai-prompts/), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-21** [grounded] There's An AI For That, an AI tool directory, makes money mainly from newsletter sponsorships sold by an in-house team, at about $6K–8K per placement, with roughly 1.7 million subscribers as of April 2025. That's an advertising-funded model. — [beehiiv case study, 7 Apr 2025](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/theres-an-ai-for-that), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Service shapes

- **MON-24** [grounded] A freelance "GTM Validation Sprint" on Contra is a packaged two-week engagement, priced from about $2K. It runs from a founder workshop through customer-profile and pricing hypotheses to a 30-day validation plan, and ends with a session deciding whether to proceed, narrow, reposition, test or stop. It states it "does not guarantee product-market fit, customers or revenue". — [Contra: GTM Validation Sprint](https://contra.com/s/vqc3LQMm-gtm-validation-sprint-for-founder-led-b2-b-software), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-25** [grounded] A digital agency's 2026 price guide lists product-discovery formats from a two-to-three-day scoping to a two-week deep discovery, priced between roughly €2.5K and €15K before tax. It says the scoping fee is often deducted from the build price when the same agency builds the product. — [Kolonell: product discovery workshop cost 2026](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/product-discovery-workshop-cost-2026), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-26** [grounded] Strategyzer released the Business Model Canvas under a Creative Commons license, so anyone may use it, consultants included, with attribution. It keeps the Value Proposition Canvas under copyright, and software use of it needs permission. Strategyzer earns from masterclasses and bootcamps, consulting, a software platform, books, and annual playbook subscriptions. — [Strategyzer: usage of our tools](https://strategyzer.com/legal/usage-of-our-tools); [Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Evidence that monetizing a prompt or skill pack is hard

- **MON-27** [grounded] An Agent Skill is a directory holding a `SKILL.md` file of YAML frontmatter and plain-text instructions, plus optional reference files and scripts. Anthropic published Agent Skills as an open standard on 18 December 2025. — [Anthropic: equipping agents with Agent Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-28** [grounded] A study of more than 200 custom GPTs reports a 97.2% success rate at extracting their system prompts and 100% at leaking their uploaded files through prompt injection. — [Yu et al., Assessing Prompt Injection Risks in 200+ Custom GPTs (arXiv 2311.11538v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11538v2), accessed 2026-10-21.
- **MON-29** [grounded] Two vendor-published guides say files are easy to copy. An agent-hosting vendor says piracy is trivial once a skill folder is downloaded, and argues for selling hosted access, since "Users consume outcomes, not your implementation." A checkout vendor relays seller comments that a buyer can post a prompt publicly or ask a model to write a similar one. — [Agent37: Claude skills marketplace, 19 Jul 2026](https://www.agent37.com/blog/claude-skills-marketplace-buy-sell-discover-skills); [CartMango: how to sell AI prompts](https://cartmango.com/sell-ai-prompts/), accessed 2026-10-21.

## Notes for frame

_Judgment, not evidence._

- Paid idea-validation tools mostly charge per report or credit pack rather than by subscription (MON-01, MON-02, MON-03). The one subscription example also bundles community and live programs (MON-05). None found relies on advertising (MON-30).
- The open-source workflow packs closest to this tool don't charge for the pack. They earn from what surrounds it: sponsorship (MON-06), a membership with courses (MON-08), enterprise support (MON-09), or a separate hosted product, protected by a license clause (MON-10). Corporate backing is another path (MON-07).
- The Claude, Copilot and Cursor marketplaces offer no payment rails (MON-12), and Anthropic's directory requires a public repository (MON-13). Selling the pack itself would happen off-marketplace or through a hosted component. Marketplaces that do share revenue exist elsewhere (MON-17, MON-18, MON-19).
- The copy-ability evidence (MON-27, MON-28, MON-29) and low prompt prices (MON-20) suggest a paid offer would rest on something files can't carry: hosted research, data, community, or time.
- Human-run validation sprints and discovery workshops are the most established paid form of the same job. They're priced in the thousands and often lead into build work (MON-24, MON-25, MON-26).
- If the tool stays free and open source, most maintainers in comparable positions are unpaid, and donations reach only a quarter of them (MON-11).
